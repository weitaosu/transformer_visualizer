A no-prerequisites walkthrough.

The problem: words don't make sense alone

Read this sentence. To understand the word "it", you have to look at other words. That's the whole idea of attention.

Attention is just a soft database lookup. Every word in the sentence does this same search — for itself, against everyone else.

Multi-Head: many viewpoints at once

One attention pattern can only catch one kind of relationship. Real transformers run several attention "heads" in parallel, each free to specialize.
6

Masking: don't peek at the future

When the model is generating text one word at a time, it must not "see" words that haven't been generated yet — otherwise it cheats during training. We mask the future.
Mask future tokens (causal)
7

Positional Encoding: who comes first?

Attention itself is order-blind: it would treat "dog bites man" the same as "man bites dog". So we add a position-fingerprint to every word — a unique pattern of sines and cosines.
Why sin/cos?
Each position gets a unique fingerprint at many wavelengths. The model can compare positions just by looking at the pattern.
PE(pos, 2i)  = sin(pos / 100002i / d)
PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos(pos / 100002i / d)

10

The whole picture, in one line

All of the math you just watched fits in a single equation. Everything else is plumbing.
Scaled Dot-Product Attention
Attention(Q, K, V)  =  softmax( Q K⊤√dk ) V
From Attention Is All You Need, Vaswani et al. 2017.

Key takeaways

  • Q = "what am I looking for?", K = "what am I?", V = "info I carry."
  • Every word does this lookup against every other word — O(n²) in sequence length.
  • Self-attention: Q, K, V all from the same sentence. Cross-attention: Q from decoder, K/V from encoder.
  • Mask the future when generating one word at a time, so the model can't peek.
  • Add a positional encoding because raw attention is order-blind.
  • Multi-head = the same attention done many times in parallel, each head free to specialize.
  • Residual connections + LayerNorm wrap every sub-layer to keep training stable.
